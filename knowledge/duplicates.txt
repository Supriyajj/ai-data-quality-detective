Duplicate rows are records that are identical (or near-identical) to another
record in the dataset. They typically enter a dataset through repeated form
submissions, retried API calls, merges of multiple source systems without
deduplication keys, or ETL jobs that re-run over already-processed data.

Handling strategies:
1. Exact duplicates (every column matches): safe to drop, keeping the first
   or most recent occurrence depending on which is more trustworthy.
2. Key-based duplicates (same ID, different other values): these usually
   indicate an update was captured as a new row instead of an edit - resolve
   by keeping the most recent timestamp, not by simple deletion.
3. Fuzzy duplicates (near-identical, e.g. "Jon Smith" vs "Jonathan Smith"):
   require fuzzy matching or manual review, not naive equality checks.

Before deduplicating, confirm whether the duplication is a data error or a
legitimate repeated event (e.g. a customer placing two identical orders on
the same day is not a "duplicate row" in the data-quality sense).
