Outliers are values that lie unusually far from the rest of the
distribution. They are not automatically "wrong" - some are legitimate rare
events (a genuinely high earner in a salary column), while others indicate
data entry or measurement error (a salary that is off by a factor of 1000
due to a currency or unit mistake).

The Interquartile Range (IQR) method is a simple, distribution-agnostic way
to flag candidates:
  Q1 = 25th percentile, Q3 = 75th percentile
  IQR = Q3 - Q1
  Lower bound = Q1 - 1.5 x IQR
  Upper bound = Q3 + 1.5 x IQR
Any value outside [lower bound, upper bound] is flagged as a statistical
outlier. This is a heuristic, not a certainty - always review flagged points
before removing them.

Other detection approaches (for reference, not required for a first version):
Z-score thresholds, modified Z-score using median absolute deviation (more
robust to extreme values than the mean-based Z-score), and model-based
methods like isolation forests for multivariate outliers.

Handling: for confirmed data errors, correct or remove the row. For
legitimate extreme values, keep them but consider whether the downstream
model needs a transform (e.g. log-scaling a heavily skewed salary column)
rather than removing real signal.
